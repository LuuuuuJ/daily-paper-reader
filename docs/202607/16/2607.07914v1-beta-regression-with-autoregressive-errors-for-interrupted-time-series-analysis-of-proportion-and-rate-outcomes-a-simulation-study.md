## 摘要
尽管比例和率结果具有有界性，但间断时间序列分析（ITSA）通常仍使用普通最小二乘回归进行估计。当使用适用于有界结果的方法时，标准方法是采用具有异方差和自相关稳健（HAC）标准误的准似然广义线性模型（GLM）。然而，现有的估计量均无法对 Beta 分布的条件密度和自回归（AR）误差结构进行联合建模。我们介绍了 betark，这是一个基于递归代入的 Beta 回归与 AR(k) 误差联合条件极大似然估计量的 Stata 实现，该方法可产生具有任意阶自回归依赖性的闭式条件 Beta 似然函数。与两阶段法不同，betark 在单个似然函数中联合估计均值、精度和 AR(k) 系数，因此报告的标准误直接考虑了自相关，无需单独校正。一项蒙特卡洛研究在单组 ITSA 设计中，针对 AR(1)-AR(3) 过程、三种序列长度和四种效应量，将 betark 与使用 Newey-West HAC 标准误的准二项式 GLM 进行了比较。两种方法基本上都是无偏的，但在大多数情况下，betark 比 GLM+HAC 产生了校准更佳的推断，在高度持久的自相关下增益最大，此时短序列的 GLM+HAC 第一类错误超过了 60%。错误指定一阶 AR 阶数以及改变起始均值和干预前趋势对性能的影响较小。然而，即使对于所研究的最长序列，在高度持久的 AR(3) 过程下，betark 自身的第一类错误仍然较高。

## Abstract
Interrupted time series analyses (ITSA) of proportion and rate outcomes are frequently estimated using ordinary least squares regression despite the bounded nature of these outcomes. When methods appropriate for bounded outcomes are used, the standard approach is a quasi-likelihood generalized linear model (GLM) with heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent (HAC) standard errors. However, no existing estimator jointly models the beta-distributed conditional density and autoregressive (AR) error structure. We introduce betark, a Stata implementation of a joint conditional maximum likelihood estimator for beta regression with AR(k) errors based on a recursive substitution that yields a closed-form conditional beta likelihood with autoregressive dependence of arbitrary order. Unlike two-stage approaches, betark jointly estimates the mean, precision, and AR(k) coefficients in a single likelihood, so reported standard errors directly account for autocorrelation without separate correction. A Monte Carlo study compared betark with a quasi-binomial GLM using Newey-West HAC standard errors across AR(1)-AR(3) processes, three series lengths, and four effect sizes in a single-group ITSA design. Both methods were essentially unbiased, but betark produced better-calibrated inference than GLM+HAC in most scenarios, with the largest gains under highly persistent autocorrelation, where GLM+HAC Type I error exceeded 60% for short series. Misspecifying the AR order by one lag and varying the starting mean and pre-intervention trend had only modest effects on performance. However, betark's own Type I error remained elevated under highly persistent AR(3) processes even for the longest series examined.
