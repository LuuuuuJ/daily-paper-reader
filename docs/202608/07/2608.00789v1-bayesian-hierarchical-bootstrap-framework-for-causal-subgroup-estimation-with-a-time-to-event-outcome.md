## 摘要
在预定义的亚组（如生物标志物定义的层级、疾病表型或人口统计学群体）内进行治疗效应的因果估计通常具有临床意义。贝叶斯方法在亚组效应估计中极具吸引力，因为灵活的先验可以表征复杂的治疗异质性并传递后验不确定性。针对具有事件发生时间结局的预定义因果亚组分析，频率学派方法也已存在，包括强调亚组水平协变量平衡的倾向评分加权法。在后验 g-公式中，亚组生存估计量还取决于亚组特有的基线协变量分布，当某些亚组规模较小或代表性不均时，这种分布可能不稳定。右删失会独立减少估计量中结局模型部分的信息，从而增加亚组因果生存对比的整体不确定性。我们将层次贝叶斯自助法（HBB）扩展到具有右删失事件发生时间结局的亚组因果推断。HBB 在亚组特有的基线协变量分布上设置了非参数层次先验，从而能够在保留亚组特有结构的同时，在相关亚组之间进行有原则的信息借用。我们将这种分布正则化与贝叶斯加速失效时间模型及右删失相结合，执行后验 g-公式，以传递来自生存模型和亚组协变量分布的不确定性。由此产生的框架在不对协变量分布施加参数假设的情况下，稳定了稀疏层中的亚组因果生存估计量。模拟研究考察了在不同程度的亚组稀疏性和删失情况下的性能，并将所提方法与用于异质生存效应的流行贝叶斯加性回归树进行了比较。

## Abstract
Causal estimation of treatment effects within prespecified subgroups, such as biomarker-defined strata, disease phenotypes, or demographic groups are often of clinical interest. Bayesian approaches are attractive for subgroup effect estimation because flexible priors can represent complex treatment heterogeneity and propagate posterior uncertainty. Frequentist methods for prespecified causal subgroup analysis with time-to-event outcomes are also available, including propensity-score weighting approaches that emphasize subgroup-level covariate balance. In the posterior g-formula, subgroup survival estimands also depend on the subgroup-specific distribution of baseline covariates, which may be unstable when some subgroups are small or unevenly represented. Right censoring separately reduces information for the outcome-model component of the estimand, increasing overall uncertainty in subgroup causal survival contrasts. We extend the hierarchical Bayesian bootstrap (HBB) to subgroup causal inference with right-censored time-to-event outcomes. The HBB places a nonparametric hierarchical prior on subgroup-specific baseline covariate distributions, enabling principled borrowing of information across related subgroups while preserving subgroup-specific structure. We combine this distributional regularization with a Bayesian accelerated failure time model and right censoring to perform a posterior g-formula that propagates uncertainty from both the survival model and the subgroup covariate distribution. The resulting framework stabilizes subgroup causal survival estimands in sparse strata without imposing parametric assumptions on the covariate distribution. Simulation studies examine performance across varying degrees of subgroup sparsity and censoring and compare the proposed approach to the popular Bayesian additive regression tree for heterogeneous survival effects.
